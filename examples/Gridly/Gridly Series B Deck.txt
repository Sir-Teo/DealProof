Slide 2 — The Problem

Commercial buildings are responsible for 36% of U.S. electricity consumption. Most of them pay peak-demand electricity rates that are 3–5x off-peak rates. A typical Class A office building in California, Texas, or New York spends $800,000–$2.4M per year on electricity, with 40–60% of that bill attributable to demand charges — fees utilities levy based on the 15-minute peak consumption window each month.

Battery storage systems can eliminate demand charges by discharging during peak windows, turning a capital expense into a predictable operating cost reduction. The technology works. The problem is economic: behind-the-meter battery systems cost $1,200–$1,800/kWh installed, and commercial building owners have 5–10% NOI margins with no appetite for large upfront capital commitments.

Gridly solves this with a battery-as-a-service model: we install, own, and operate the battery. The building pays a monthly fee below their current demand charge expense. Positive cash flow from day one for the building owner, no capex.

Slide 4 — The Model

Gridly installs 200–800 kWh lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery systems in commercial buildings (office, retail, light industrial, multifamily). Gridly owns the hardware. We charge a monthly service fee equal to 80% of the building's trailing 6-month average demand charge. The building saves 20% of demand charges immediately, and more as our dispatch algorithm optimizes over time.

Revenue streams:
1. Monthly service fees (primary, recurring): average $14,200/building/month ($170K/building/year)
2. Demand response revenue: grid operators (PJM, CAISO, ERCOT) pay Gridly for using our fleet to balance grid load. Incremental revenue of $8,000–$22,000/building/year depending on market.
3. Solar integration premium: buildings that add rooftop solar route through our battery, increasing solar utilization and adding $3,000–$6,000/year per site.

Unit economics per site:
- Hardware cost: $185,000 installed (250 kWh system, typical commercial installation)
- Installation + commissioning: $28,000
- Total CapEx per site: $213,000
- Annual service fee revenue: $170,400 (average)
- Annual demand response revenue: $14,000 (average)
- O&M per site per year: $9,200
- Site-level cash payback: 4.9 years
- Site-level IRR: 18.2%

Fleet-level economics:
- 312 sites deployed as of December 2024
- Fleet ARR: $53.2M (service fees + demand response)
- Gross margin: 61% at fleet level

Slide 10 — Competitive Landscape

Stem (NASDAQ: STEM): Public company, $1.3B market cap. Software-only, partners with hardware vendors. Struggling with profitability; announced layoffs in Q3 2024. Their model requires customer capex — our model does not. We see them in about 15% of deals.

Enchanted Rock: Microgrid-focused, natural gas backup generation. Different use case (backup power, not demand charge reduction). Not a direct competitor.

Omnidian: Operations and maintenance for solar+storage. Services company, not a BaaS provider. Complementary, not competitive.

In-house solutions (DIY): Large REITs sometimes procure their own batteries through Tesla Powerpack or BYD. Challenge: they then own O&M, dispatch software, and grid interconnection complexity. We see this in about 10% of large enterprise deals; win rate is low because the economics favor our model unless the REIT has dedicated energy staff.

Tesla Energy: Powerpack deployed at commercial scale, but Tesla is not offering BaaS — they sell hardware. Gridly partners with Tesla hardware on some sites.

We have no direct BaaS-model commercial storage competitor at scale in the U.S. The closest analog is Swell Energy (acquired by Osaka Gas in 2021) which focused on residential; the commercial market is less competitive.
